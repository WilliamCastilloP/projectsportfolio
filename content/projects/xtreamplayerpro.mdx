## Context

Xtream Codes portals expose thousands of channels through a thin JSON API, and
most clients render that firehose as one endless list. XtreamPlayerPro treats
the portal as a catalogue instead: everything is grouped, searchable, and
reachable without leaving the keyboard.

## What I built

- A typed API layer over the Xtream Codes endpoints, so a malformed portal
  response fails loudly at the boundary rather than halfway through a render.
- An EPG view that virtualises both axes — channels down, time across — and
  keeps the current programme anchored while you scroll ahead.
- A player shell around HLS.js with its own buffering and error states, because
  the stream quality varies far more than the UI should.
- Persisted favourites and a resume position per channel, stored locally.

<Note>
  The portal credentials never leave the machine: everything is requested
  directly from the client, and nothing is proxied through a server I control.
</Note>

## Notable details

Channel switching is the interaction that matters most, so it gets the budget:
the next stream is prepared before the current one is torn down, and the UI
holds the previous frame until the new one can paint. The result reads as a cut
rather than a flash of black.
