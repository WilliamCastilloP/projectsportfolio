## Context

Splitting a trip evenly is easy. Splitting it when one person books the flights,
another pays for a week of groceries in a different currency, and a third joins
halfway through is where every shared spreadsheet falls apart.

## What I built

- A ledger where an expense records who paid, who it covers, and the rate at the
  time it was entered — the rate is stored, not recalculated, so history stays
  stable.
- Balances derived from the ledger rather than stored, which means an edit or a
  deletion can never leave the totals inconsistent.
- A settle-up step that reduces the debt graph to the smallest set of transfers.
- Offline-tolerant entry: expenses queue locally and sync when the connection
  comes back, which is the normal state of affairs abroad.

<Note>
  Every mutation goes through a Zod schema shared by the client form and the
  server action, so the two can't drift.
</Note>

## Notable details

The hard part was never the maths — it was making the maths legible. Each
balance links back to the exact entries that produced it, so anyone can audit
their own number instead of trusting the total.
